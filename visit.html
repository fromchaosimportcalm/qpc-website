🙏
You won't be put on the spot

No one will ask you to stand up, introduce yourself, or do anything you're not comfortable with. Come and simply observe if that's what you need.

👔
Come as you are

There’s no set dress code—some dress up, others come more casually. We want you to feel welcome, and we also value dressing in a way that shows respect for God and for one another.

👧
Kids are genuinely welcome

Children's ministry runs during both morning services. Babies and toddlers are welcome in the service too — a little noise is fine. We were all young once.

☕
Stay for a cuppa

After each service there's morning tea. It's the best part for getting to know people. No pressure to stay, but we hope you will.

Step by Step

What to expect
on Sunday morning

01
Arrive a few minutes early

The building is at 2 Morisset Street, set back from the road among trees adjacent to Queanbeyan Park. Aim to arrive 5–10 minutes before the service — it gives you time to find a seat and say hello to someone.

02
You'll be welcomed at the door

Someone will greet you as you come in and give you a service sheet. They'll point you to seats and answer any questions. If you mention it's your first time, they'll make sure you're looked after.

03
The service runs about 60 minutes

We sing together (hymns that have stood the test of time), pray, read from the Bible, and listen to an expository sermon — teaching that works through a passage of Scripture and connects it to everyday life. The sermons themselves tend to be around 20 minutes.

04
Kids head to their own programme

Children are welcome to stay in the service the whole time, but there's also a children's ministry programme that runs during the sermon. A leader will guide kids out at the right moment — no need to organise anything in advance.

05
Stay for morning tea

After the service there's tea, coffee, and good conversation. It's informal and relaxed. This is where you'll meet people properly, and where someone will likely remember your name next week.

Good to Know
Do I need a Bible?

No — the service sheet will include the Bible passage being preached. Bibles are available in the pews if you'd like to follow along (page numbers are referenceed during the reading). We use the NIV translation.

Good to Know
Which service should I come to?

Both services have the same format and the same sermon. Come to whichever suits your morning.

Good to Know
Will there be a collection?

There is an offering during the service. As a visitor you're under no obligation whatsoever, simply pass the plate along. Giving is for members of the congregation.

Good to Know
What if I have questions about faith?

Bring them. No question is too basic or too hard. After the service is a good time to chat with the minister or an elder... they genuinely enjoy those conversations.

Common Questions

Things people
wonder about

A few questions we hear often — especially from people considering coming for the first time.

Ask us something else
Is this a church for people who already believe? +

Not exclusively. We have members who have followed Christ for decades, people who are exploring faith for the first time, and everyone in between. Wherever you are on that journey, you're welcome. The sermon is aimed at everyone — not just the convinced.

What is "Presbyterian"? Is it very different from other churches? +

Presbyterian describes our form of church governance — led by a team of elders rather than a single pastor — and our theological tradition, which is Reformed (rooted in the Reformation and the Westminster Confession of Faith). In practice, the service is straightforward: singing, prayer, Bible reading, and a sermon. No liturgy, no ritual, nothing unfamiliar.

I haven't been to church in years. Will that be obvious? +

Not at all. There's a service sheet that explains what's happening as the service proceeds. Nobody will know or care how long it's been — what matters is that you're there now.

Do I need to be a member to take communion? +

Communion is for those who are baptised and professing Christians. If you're a visitor and unsure whether to participate, it's completely fine to let the elements pass — no one will think less of you. If you'd like to discuss it, the minister is happy to chat after the service.

What about my children — can they sit with me? +

Absolutely. Children are welcome to stay in the service for the whole time. There's also a children's programme that runs during the sermon for kids who'd like something more age-appropriate. You choose what works for your family — and if a toddler makes noise, that's just fine.

Is there anything I need to bring or do to prepare? +

Nothing at all. Just show up. A curious and open mind is the only thing required. If you want to read the passage beforehand, it'll be listed on this website — but that's entirely optional.
